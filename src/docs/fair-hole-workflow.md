# Fair Hole workflow

Grid probing and live replay use the same hidden-boundary policy. Vertices shared with hidden faces are carried into the visible-only probe as protected metadata: neither path dissolves, welds, slides, or completes triangles through those vertices. Manhattan side analysis counts only visible incident edges. This keeps hidden topology intact without copying hidden faces into the fit, and prevents a successful probe from taking different preparation decisions during replay. If these constraints prevent a complete Grid, Auto can fall back to Circle as usual. Temporary probe construction and mapping failures also take the guarded fallback path and release their allocated BMesh.

Fair Hole discovers one open boundary using connectivity alone. **Fit** and **Topology** default to **Auto Fit** and **Auto Topology**. The dropdowns beside the sidebar button show Fit first and Topology second and preselect these choices for each new invocation; changing redo values does not change the next invocation. Auto Topology tries a complete Manhattan grid first on spherical and organic fits. If grid preparation/planning fails, it fills the original boundary with five circular rings instead.

## Boundary selection

The mouse is tested against projected edges with exactly one linked face. A hit seeds a connectivity flood over neighboring one-face edges; face-normal angle does not participate. The component must contain at least three edges, every vertex must have degree two within the component, and one ordered walk must visit every edge before returning to the seed. Open chains, branches, and connected multiple loops are rejected.

The complete component is drawn as a screen-space line overlay. Clicking or pressing Enter confirms it. Esc restores the pre-invocation mesh selection.

## Inspectable Grid steps

Click Fair Hole and confirm the boundary to fill the complete hole. Normal builds expose only **Match Curvature (C2)** as a grid-specific option, checked by default; the full patch runs with 20 relaxation passes at strength 0.5. A build compiled with `--development-controls` exposes **Stop After**, row limits, relaxation settings, and diagnostics in redo. The post-mirror pass count is hard-coded to one in both builds.

1. **Dissolve two-edge vertices.** Walk the selected border and dissolve vertices with exactly two connected mesh edges, not merely two edges within the loop. No angle threshold is used. Vertices with any additional edge remain intact. The loop is rediscovered through its surviving live vertices after each dissolve; stored element indices and nearest-position searches do not choose the modified region. A triangular support face or a loop of only three vertices is retained rather than destroyed.
2. **Repair pentagon corners.** After Step 1, gather the faces incident to the surviving ring edges and merge all vertices belonging to those faces by distance at **0.001 mesh units**, including their outer support vertices but excluding vertices outside that face region, then validate/reorder the surviving loop before inspecting five-sided faces with exactly one edge exposed to the chosen opening. Treat that edge as a clipped quad corner: extend each endpoint's other edge in that pentagon toward the hole, intersect the two directions in world space, slide the endpoints to that intersection, and weld them into one vertex. The exposed edge disappears and the pentagon becomes a quad. This is not a midpoint collapse of the exposed edge. Pentagon repair leaves the remaining source vertices fixed.
3. **Slide and refine T points.** After Steps 1–2, finish the quad boundary alignment pass before processing triangle-touching vertices. A quad-supported vertex with exactly one non-boundary edge slides outward along its extension toward **90° between its edge toward the nearest corrected boundary vertex and its inward edge into the completed mesh**. Repaired pentagon corners and fixed quad junctions seed the corrected region; successful slides extend it. Choose the nearest corrected anchor along a quad-supported boundary run, never using triangle or unrepaired ngon edges as references. Triangle-touching vertices then receive their boundary-ordered initial turning-angle continuation. After these initial slides, run **three T-only perimeter refinement passes** on all three-edge boundary vertices with one non-boundary spoke, including those touching triangles. For ordinary T points without adjacent triangles, bidirectional spoke slides minimize the full T-shape error: the two border branches approach 180° while both approach 90° to the inward spoke. At a triangle-adjacent T point, target 90° jointly at the movable corner and both neighboring corners of the completed-side quad, without using the unfinished triangle-side branch. These points are free in both directions within the plane of the quad's other three corners, not restricted to their original spoke line. A parallelogram seed and bounded two-dimensional pattern search minimize the three changing angle errors and nonplanarity; an already-right movable corner does not stop refinement if its neighbors are still skewed. Both-triangle cases lack an unambiguous completed side and stay unchanged. A T point diagonally opposite a repaired pentagon corner across an adjacent quad is also free in both planar directions. Its other three quad corners define the plane, and rectangularity supplies the objective; the repaired vertex itself stays locked. Only actual pentagon-repair identities enable this exception, not arbitrary locked or nearby vertices. Step 3 still makes three full perimeter refinement walks after initial alignment. Ordinary line searches use 33 samples and 28 search iterations; free planar quad fits use 12 pattern-search iterations per pass. Repaired pentagon corners remain locked. No triangles are completed in Step 3.

4. **Refined Edge.** Complete the bowtie and mirrored-triangle patterns using Step 3's three T-only passes, then run exactly one fixed post-mirror pass. Ordinary T points slide along their non-boundary spoke. Points diagonally opposite repaired pentagon corners and triangle-adjacent points retain their free planar quad fit. Mirrored-triangle corners optimize both planar coordinates to reduce angle errors at the movable corner and its two neighbors, plus nonplanarity. Each walk uses preceding vertices' updated positions. Every move must improve its objective and preserve orientation, usable area, and a non-crossing boundary; repaired pentagon corners remain fixed. After refinement, a three-edge U-shaped run bounded by two inside corners is capped with one quad using its four existing vertices. Both middle corners must be multi-edge source junctions, the quad must be convex, and its closing chord/interior must stay inside the opening. No vertices or edge cuts are added. Only runs from the incoming perimeter are considered; this pass does not recursively fill rows. The panel reports **Single-cell notch caps**. Step 4 leaves the refined opening available for development inspection.

For a **bowtie**, the two exposed boundary triangles share one vertex but no edge. Add one new vertex toward the missing corner and insert it into both triangles' exposed edges, producing **two quads sharing the new edge from the old shared vertex**. The original shared vertex becomes interior to the repaired surface. The new vertex lies in both triangle planes: coplanar triangles permit a planar corner; distinct planes constrain it to their intersection line through the old shared vertex. Position this new middle boundary vertex to minimize the turning angle between its two edges toward the neighboring rim vertices, aiming for a straight border (180° interior angle, zero turning angle), not a parallelogram corner. A flat bowtie first tries the chord midpoint. On distinct planes, a bounded search minimizes the turn along their intersection line, since projecting the midpoint alone can miss the optimum on an asymmetric bowtie. Every candidate must produce two convex quads and remain inside the opening without crossings. Post-mirror passes retain this boundary-turn objective for the tracked new vertex rather than pulling it toward a rectangular corner; existing neighboring vertices are not moved by the repair itself. A bowtie that branches the entire boundary into multiple loops still fails the initial simple-loop validation.

For a **triangle with one exposed boundary edge**, rotate its opposite vertex 180° about the face normal through the boundary-edge midpoint in world space. With edge endpoints A and B and opposite vertex C, the new corner is A + B − C. This completes a parallelogram, rectangular when the source triangle is right-angled, rather than reflecting C across the edge. Neighboring faces may be triangles or n-gons, not just quads; two edge-adjacent triangles are completed independently while preserving their shared edge. Insert the new corner into the exposed edge to replace each triangle with a quad, keeping all original vertex positions unchanged. The old exposed edge is removed rather than left as a wire or diagonal. Crossing or out-of-opening completions remain rejected, and triangles with multiple exposed edges have no unique edge for this operation. The shared-vertex bowtie repair above remains a separate joint completion.

Triangle completion requires convex, correctly oriented quads and new border segments contained in the remaining opening. A bowtie also requires a useful common spoke: its mesh-space length must exceed the existing 0.001 merge distance, its world-space length must exceed 10% of the median source-triangle edge length, and it must improve the rim turn. Sharing a boundary vertex is not sufficient to couple two triangles: if their safe half-turn targets are well separated and point more than 45° apart from that vertex, complete the two source cells independently. Near-coincident or same-spoke targets retain joint bowtie repair, including asymmetric and creased pairs. Some curved triangle pairs have intersecting planes that admit only a nearly zero common spoke; they are instead completed individually by half-turns, not disguised as collapsed quads. An unavailable bowtie is not reported as a skipped triangle if individual completion succeeds. Unsafe or ambiguous individual completions are skipped. Materials, face smoothing/custom attributes, and existing UV coordinates are retained; the new corner's UVs are extrapolated from the original triangle. Step 4 preserves those UV values while refining the positions.

After pentagon repair, and again after Step 3 refinement before triangle completion, inspect connected edges in the selected ring faces for lengths at most 0.001 in mesh space. For quad rim-to-support spokes only, a relative limit of 16% of the quad's second-longest edge also detects collapsed corners that exceed the absolute threshold. After these neighboring repairs, repeat the two-edge-vertex dissolve with repaired and protected corners locked; a formerly ambiguous pentagon can then become a proper quad. A collapsed quad corner welds to its repaired fixed corner when present, otherwise to the existing support vertex. This converts almost-triangular quads back into actual triangles before classifying them. Only connected edges touching the live rim are candidates, not arbitrary nearby vertex pairs or stored indices. Pinched links, lost faces, inverted normals/corners, and merges between two fixed repaired corners are rejected. Live boundary and repaired-corner identities are updated after every weld. The panel reports **Collapsed corner edges merged** separately from the initial ring-face merge. The saved sphere regression completes 21 triangles independently and fills its 23/20/23/20 refined boundary with 354 quads, without cuts or mutations.

Step 2 skips ambiguous pentagons with multiple exposed edges, nearly parallel or non-rectangular corner directions, skew lines without a close intersection, intersections behind the source directions or too far away, and merges that would fold or collapse the resulting quad or an incident face. Near intersections use the midpoint between the two closest points. Their gap allowance is four times the local geometric tolerance, with a float32 mesh/world-transform precision floor capped at 0.1% of the local face extent. Only this gap test is relaxed; length, convexity, orientation, distance, and neighboring-face checks retain their previous thresholds. The panel reports dissolved vertices, ring-face vertices merged at 0.001, repaired pentagons, and skipped pentagons. Step 3 additionally reports initial slides and accepted moves for each of the three pre-mirror T passes. Step 4 reports bowtie repairs, completed triangles, skipped slide/triangle cases, and accepted moves for each post-mirror pass.

Steps 1–4 do not construct a complete infill, globally project the surface, merge unrelated geometry by distance, dissolve the remaining hole boundary, or run Final Simplify. Step 4 locally expands existing triangles into quads and caps eligible single-cell U notches and leaves the Refined Edge selected. Step 5 fills this boundary without density controls or additional post-mirror refinement; even-length refined boundaries are kept intact, and only an odd perimeter needs one parity cut to retain all-quads.

## Step 5: Fill Quad Rows

The Refined Edge is the patcher's input, not the old circular border. The source-edge folded SVD supplies the projection frame, and every existing boundary vertex is a hard positional constraint. Grid now always uses the in-house **Manhattan Grid** template; the obsolete native, cross-field, and local backends have been removed, and no native executable is required. At **Step 4: Refined Edge**, inspect **U1 / V1 / U2 / V2** and the template size without filling. These sides are interlaced direction groups, not four contiguous perimeter chains. Near-right-angle corners are measured in source 3D coordinates: an interior corner has more than two total connected edges and turns the grid heading forward; an exterior corner has exactly two total edges and turns it backward. Straight runs keep their heading. The complete walk must have four net quarter turns, and the longest direction group becomes U1 independently of the walk's starting vertex. If the refined edge is not yet suitable, Step 4 reports the analysis issue but leaves that state available for inspection; Step 5 rejects an invalid template.

At Step 5, opposing U counts define the template width, and opposing V counts define its height. Exterior-corner intrusions trim cells that are already occupied. An integer row planner schedules equal-width strips or local three-to-one transitions, each constructed from four quads contained in a single row. The saved Suzanne Refined Edge counts **8 / 5 / 2 / 5**: its **8 × 5** template trims five encroached cells and fills with **26 quads**, **three mutation patches**, **zero triangles**, and **zero boundary cuts**. Planning stations no longer become boundary subdivisions. Unequal row widths use local three-to-one quad mutations; when the opposite wall has fewer stations, a row holds an existing endpoint while the other side advances, creating an all-quad corner transition. The count schedule enforces the appropriate parity for shared endpoints, and triangle-producing strip candidates are rejected. Uniform and alternating endpoint-hold schedules are tried in a bounded search before rejecting a sweep. Even-length refined perimeters receive zero cuts; an odd perimeter receives exactly one explicitly reported parity cut because an all-quad disk requires an even boundary edge count. Original rim vertices remain fixed. There is no density or automatic target-size control. The planner first tries the canonical longest-side sweep, then retries once perpendicular if that layout fails. A successful preferred layout is unchanged; a perpendicular layout reports its rotated side counts and template dimensions. Each sweep still requires one connected missing interval per row; if neither direction is valid, the patch reports an error rather than bridging completed geometry. The notched Suzanne regression now includes redundant-corner cleanup and one local notch cap, then retries the perpendicular sweep with a 7 × 10 template; its odd refined rim requires one reported parity cut. The current larger Suzanne opening instead has 9 / 5 / 3 / 5 sides after one cap and fills with 32 quads including that cap, three mutation patches, no triangles, and zero boundary cuts.

Manhattan interior coordinates are initially interpolated between the actual refined rim anchors. After convexity repair, **Interior Relaxation** performs boundary-locked Laplacian smoothing followed by quad-shape optimization of only new interior points. It defaults to **20** passes, allows **0–100**, and zero disables smoothing. **Relaxation Strength** defaults to **0.5**. Laplacian sweeps provide an initial placement, then damped local Newton steps minimize inverse corner quality `(length(u)^2 + length(v)^2) / (2 * cross(u,v))`. This objective penalizes both skew angles and extreme aspect ratios; merely shortening edges does not. Every accepted move decreases incident-face distortion and retains positive corners. For held-station layouts, the planner compares valid endpoint-hold patterns and geometry-aware count schedules rather than accepting the first manifold layout. Source boundary station spacing supplies the row-aspect cost, not a user density or boundary resampling; extra interior growth/reduction mutations are allowed when they improve cell shapes. Flat-chart planning locks original rim vertices and required cut positions. The subsequent surface-aware relaxation includes the refined rim, except protected vertices and parity-cut endpoints. Planning, smoothing, and chart validation finish before construction changes the live mesh. Relaxed positions are projected onto the frozen fitted surface, never onto a fit derived from newly filled faces or already-relaxed rim positions.

Relaxation also checks cell quality on the actual projected 3D surface. Flat-chart squares can become twisted or spiky quads on a steep brow, even after many 2D iterations. This final solve includes both interior vertices and the refined rim, reprojecting each trial onto the unchanged fitted surface. Vertices beyond the rim stay fixed. The attached exterior faces participate in quality and fold/collapse checks, and rim movement is limited to a local neighborhood. Vertices touching hidden faces, parity cuts, and parity-cut endpoints stay pinned. The same count/strength controls apply with C2 on or off and with analytic fits; zero disables both relaxation stages. The fit is frozen from the Refined Edge before smoothing, so the moved rim cannot feed back into it. No topology or source UV values change.

The final solver batches noninteracting vertex updates, including their projection trials and small linear solves. Organic and other general fits preserve the previous ordered update trajectory using dependency wavefronts. Sphere uses a faster multicolor sweep and vectorized analytic intersections; placement can change slightly, but topology, requested pass count, distortion objective, and all source-face/fold guards remain unchanged. Temporary batches contain at most 128 vertices. This path is shared with Resize Hole's spherical reconstruction.

**Rows to Fill** is an inspection limit: **0** fills all rows, **1** fills the first row, and larger values stop later. The panel reports filled/total rows, mutation patches, and transition triangles. New patch faces are selected, along with every remaining open front when filling only part of the patch; a stepped footprint can leave multiple independent front loops. Changing the row count in redo rebuilds from the original operator input. Surface-aware relaxation uses the complete planned patch, so the row inspection limit does not alter already-shown vertex positions.

For **Organic** Grid, **Match Curvature (C2)** is on by default. Before filling or cutting, capture the refined rim and exterior two-ring. The faces bordering the refined opening provide direct tangent constraints at their area centroids. Changes of face-plane normals over the neighboring edge-connected face ring estimate slope and curvature at the rim through a symmetric local fit. These face-derived estimates take precedence over vertex-height derivatives; insufficient or unreliable normal stencils fall back to local quadratic height estimates. Hidden faces and nearly vertical/opposite-facing planes are excluded. World-space geometry handles non-uniform scale, shear, and reflection. Area centroids and weights avoid bias from redundant edge points, and the direct border planes take priority over extrapolated derivative jets. A regularized thin-plate field with a quadratic polynomial matches rim heights as hard constraints and balances exterior heights with the estimated first/second derivatives. Planned cut positions also constrain its heights. Its kernel is `0.5 * s * log(s)` with `s = r² + 10⁻⁶` in a unit-normalized chart, giving finite, continuous second derivatives even at source points. This replaces the former `r^5` continuation, whose far-field coupling could still create a broad brow bulge despite stable curvature estimates and interior guidance. Boundary matching is approximate for slope/curvature because a polygon mesh supplies discrete, sometimes noisy samples; the resulting polygon faces themselves are not a mathematically C2 surface. The panel labels an accepted fit **C2 continuation (estimated)**. Turning the toggle off, or insufficient independent samples for curvature estimation, uses the existing frozen thin-plate continuation instead. The panel reports that fallback as **Thin plate**. Project interior points and relaxed refined-rim points along the fitted axis. Original Refined Edge samples define the frozen fit; parity cuts, their source endpoints, and exterior vertices remain fixed. Both relaxation and the fit use the complete planned patch and unchanged source, so partial-row inspection does not change already-shown positions. Planar input stays planar, while curved input follows the surrounding surface. This is an organic height-field estimate, not an analytic sphere or cylinder fit. Local curvature estimates are normalized by each neighborhood's radius and rejected when their sample layout is too narrow to constrain second derivatives reliably. Rejected corners remain exact positional boundary constraints but do not inject unstable curvature into the global surface. Useful neighboring estimates are retained; insufficient reliable neighborhoods use thin-plate fallback. This rejects unstable corner curvature without clipping interior positions or changing topology. Reliable corner estimates alone do not constrain the missing interior: the fit also uses soft, frozen thin-plate shape guidance inside the actual opening to prevent a broad upward dome. This guide reproduces exact quadratic surfaces and is sampled independently of the generated grid; it adds no mesh vertices. Gradient and curvature weights use each local stencil's radius rather than the whole hole's extent. The result remains the frozen C2 field fitted to the original rim heights, not a clipped or flattened mesh.

The planner validates positive convex faces, chart containment, complete area coverage, and conforming edge ownership before mesh construction. Invalid or infeasible patches are rejected; the Fair Hole operator restores its pre-execution Edit Mode snapshot. Rows/columns are capped at 256 and faces at 65,536. Steps 1–4 remain independently inspectable. Auto probes a standalone BMesh before touching the source boundary, so failed grid edits cannot leak into the circle fallback. Explicit Grid and partial development stages report failure instead of silently choosing circles.

The final step retains its full quality settings while avoiding duplicate work. Within each orientation search, identical compact candidate footprints share validation and fully relaxed coordinates; distinct candidates still receive the same quality comparison. Candidate reuse is limited to footprints with at most 2,048 coordinates to bound memory. Auto replays a successful probe's numeric layout, frozen fit, and relaxation result only after verifying the live refined source and settings match. Preparation and mesh construction still use live element identities. This private per-operation reuse expires immediately after the repair, so later edits always start from the current mesh.

### Folded support surfaces

Circle and Grid now use the same frozen Organic fitting entry point. If a refined border face grazes or folds across the height-chart axis (oriented normal dot product below 0.2), nearby projected points can have incompatible depths; a scalar height fit can create a ridge even without any topology relaxation. This case uses a private 3D minimum-bending reference constrained by the visible source rim and surrounding face ring, rather than forcing C2 height interpolation. The panel reports **3D bending continuation (folded chart)** for either curvature-toggle setting. Its reference triangulation and subdivisions never split source/output edges and do not depend on Circle Loops or grid row counts. Smooth cubic point/normal triangles approximate the resulting reference; this fallback is not claimed to be C2. Well-conditioned support retains the C2/thin-plate choice described above. Both evaluators are captured before any output faces exist and remain frozen during relaxation. Resize Hole reconstruction uses this same policy.

## Existing analytic fairing

If pre-fair limited dissolve removes the selected opening while leaving a valid supporting surface, the dissolve has already completed an N-plane repair. Keep that surface, report N-Plane/Plane, and skip analytic refitting and new patch construction; optional final cleanup still applies. Boundary recovery stays attached to the original ring's surviving vertices/edges and local support faces, never an unrelated opening elsewhere on the mesh. Losing the supporting geometry or producing degenerate faces remains a failure and restores the operator snapshot.

**Auto Topology** uses the plane fan for N-plane fits and axial collapse for cylinders. Spherical and organic fits attempt the complete grid before falling back to five circle rings. Cones use Circle. **Circle** retains concentric rings and a center fan on the chosen fit. **Circle Loops** sets the number of rings, including zero for a direct fan. Fit can be Auto Fit, N-Plane, Cylinder, Sphere, or Organic. Grid respects the same Fit selector as Circle. Sphere, Cylinder, and N-Plane project new interior vertices onto the accepted analytic fit along the chart axis; Organic uses the C2/thin-plate continuation from the Refined Edge. Analytic fits do not run the organic continuation or expose its C2 toggle. Topology and boundary preparation are unchanged when switching fits. Final surface-aware relaxation may move unpinned refined-rim vertices on the selected frozen fit; exterior geometry vertices and parity-cut endpoints remain fixed. A patch without interior vertices can therefore look identical for different fits; larger patches and imperfectly spherical support show the difference.

Only faces touching the chosen rim are automatic analytic-fit samples. The cascade is:

1. **N-plane intersection.** Consecutive support runs are progressively fitted and disconnected runs on the same plane are merged. Up to three distinct planes are accepted. One plane supplies a projected center, two planes an intersection line, and three planes an intersection point. Plane-normal angles below 0.4° are treated as parallel.
2. **Circular cone/cylinder and sphere.** Both fits run if planes fail. Stable fits with normalized residuals at most 3% are compared, and the lower residual wins. Forced cylinder and sphere fits retain their rank requirements but bypass the automatic residual gate.
3. **Organic.** The final hypothesis attempts the complete Manhattan grid before Circle fallback. Both topologies share frozen C2/thin-plate continuation for well-conditioned height charts and 3D bending continuation for folded support.

Non-Grid paths retain their existing 0.1° initial limited dissolve. A cylinder uses boundary-inclusive dissolve, collapses the rim only along its fitted axis, and merges at 0.0001. Sphere Circle repairs refit after boundary-inclusive pre-dissolve. Analytic ring vertices project onto their accepted surface; organic Circle vertices use the shared Organic evaluator captured before ring construction.

Before non-Grid pre-dissolve, weld collapsed connected boundary edges within 1e-8 mesh units. Coincident rim vertices otherwise create zero-area fan/ring faces because angle-based cleanup skips zero-length directions. The weld never includes spatially nearby disconnected geometry. Triangulated duplicate corners can shed their existing zero-area ears; valid triangles and support faces are preserved, with unsafe topology changes rejected. This cleanup is shared with Resize Hole reconstruction.

**Final Simplify** applies only to completed non-Grid repairs. After required seam welding, the generated face region grows by one connected topological ring and receives a normal-delimited 0.1° Limited Dissolve in edge-selection context. The previous selection mode is restored. Disabling Final Simplify preserves the generated interior topology.

## Resize Hole compatibility

Resize Hole's Boolean reconstruction calls the same `fair_hole_patch` pipeline, with the same preparation, fixed post-mirror pass, full Manhattan fill, C2 toggle, and Auto fallback. Inspection stages never leave an incomplete Boolean operand. Surface hypotheses accepted from the intact support are retained; only analytic pre-dissolve requests a same-model refit. Reconstruction subdivisions remain until the Exact Boolean subtraction, then the existing tagged-surface cleanup runs. Grid angle/density and the old cross-field mesher are removed.

## Commit and failure behavior

Changes occur only after confirmation. The operator snapshots the mesh before editing; invalid topology or an operation failure restores that snapshot. Successful Grid preparation selects the surviving boundary, updates normals, and records Undo without claiming that the hole was filled. Non-Grid repairs select the completed patch and report the accepted fit.
